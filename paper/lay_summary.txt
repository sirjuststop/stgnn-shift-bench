Traffic prediction systems help cities anticipate congestion, but a model that works well in routine tests may behave differently when sensors fail, when it is moved to another city, or when weekday traffic gives way to weekend traffic. We compare eight forecasting systems under these changes while keeping their trained parameters fixed. Their rankings depend on the kind of change: there is no single winner across all settings. We also find that concentrating information flow on a few sensors is associated with greater sensitivity to missing sensor readings. An adjustment that accounts for missing sensors substantially reduces this sensitivity in one model without retraining. Finally, the way prediction errors are measured can reverse conclusions about weekday and weekend performance. These findings help explain when a familiar accuracy ranking may be unreliable for deployment.
